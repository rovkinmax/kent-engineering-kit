# Objective

Prepare safe successor Tasks for `$ARGUMENTS` on the selected installed workflow.
Preserve useful work and original authority while replacing obsolete execution
infrastructure. Preparation does not authorize creation, launch, source transfer,
publication, predecessor completion or deletion.
Preparation ends at the preview. Execute only after verifiable approval of that
exact preview and its enumerated effects.

# Discover

Investigate each predecessor with bounded read-only inspection; parallelize
independent investigations only with available authorized roles. Independently
review the migration proposal as required by project governance.
Resolve current CLI/server, workflow and source bindings
from evidence, not assumed latest versions.

For each predecessor record:
- Related replacement, successor and diagnostic Tasks and their accepted
  results. Investigate the continuation chain, not only the stale source Task;
  do not recreate work completed elsewhere or infer native closure automatically.
- Required outcome, exclusions, original human decision locators and unanswered
  decisions. Distinguish requirements from proposed implementation means.
- Original baseline, current HEAD, index/worktree delta and untracked product
  files; separate generated/cache/evidence files. A new branch does not carry
  uncommitted work.
- Accepted and working plans, completed work and concrete remaining acceptance.
- Execution receipts and source/environment bindings, stale evidence and UNKNOWN
  effects. Historical success is not current-source admission.
- Consumed attempts, technical limits, resources, ownership and cleanup duties.
- Incoming and outgoing Task dependencies, blockers and the effect of closing
  the predecessor; preserve required blocking relationships through successors.
- Relevant sibling-platform implementations with immutable references and
  explicit adopted/rejected differences, not inferred feature authority.
  Keep sibling research conditional on relevant references or a concrete gap.

Classify each original decision as applicable unchanged, requiring clarification,
or unanswered; check branch/source/Task/environment/resource bindings. Historical
approvals and accepted plans are not native successor acceptance.

# Consolidate

Choose target workflow/source/storage from supported project admission. Keep
the original comparison baseline separate from the new integration target.
Do not copy obsolete helpers or legacy storage into a new Task by default.
Check that the target route supports the remaining work, including no-diff
diagnostics and negative test outcomes where needed. Do not fabricate source
changes to satisfy a gate. Inspect copied tests/reporters for embedded old Task
identity; propose adaptations separately without rewriting old receipts.

Check the route beyond initial transfer: normal continuation, revalidation and
recovery after approval. Compare required transition outputs, producer and
consumer instructions, and deterministic guard contracts. A generated or
installed graph is not proof that its prompts agree with its parameter schema.
Distinguish explicitly carried values from values restored by an authoritative
guard; do not add undeclared outputs or drop required routing authority.
For retained execution, include locked instructions and pending approval
snapshots, not only the current graph. Record a mismatch as infrastructure
recovery work under its own authority; never turn it into product repair.

Prepare a self-contained successor body with outcome, scope, progress,
remaining work, original decisions, verification requirements and blockers.
Write for a human reader: short Goal, Already done, Remaining work and
Acceptance sections. Use concrete behavior and readable bullets; avoid UUID
inventories and protocol dumps in the main narrative. Put exact source manifests,
decision locators and transfer checks in a linked durable technical handoff,
explicitly required before planning or mutation. Preserve material restrictions
in the readable body; links must not hide a contradictory authority boundary.
Reference durable evidence; summaries never replace original consent.
Identify exact commits/files to transfer and which historical work will not
transfer. Distinguish deliberate loss from unknown recoverability.
Use four dispositions: transfer, historical context, deliberately omit, unknown.
Acceptable progress loss never includes silent loss of restrictions or effects.

Preserve restrictions across identities. A successor ID does not restore
attempts, approve pending decisions or confer ownership of old resources.
Specify how to prevent concurrent predecessor/successor writers and how old
cleanup duties remain owned.
No live Session does not prove resources are released. Do not rewrite owner IDs.

# Preview and decision

Present exact successor bodies, target pins, branch names, transfer boundaries,
predecessor disposition, ownership handling, verification and rollback.
Choose a compatible workflow before Task creation; do not assume Start can
change a previously selected workflow. Leave unsupported successors prepared/
not-created until the workflow prerequisite is resolved.
List separate effects: creation, initialization, transfer, execution, dependency
updates, predecessor cancellation, publication and eventual cleanup. Obtain
approval for the intended effects only.
State exactly what accepting the migration handoff permits next and what
remains deferred. Honor applicable existing decisions instead of asking the
same permission again. Source-bound verification or a genuinely new effect
may still need a decision; explain that difference explicitly. A resumed
Session or removed approval alone is not proof of human scope authorization:
verify the original decision and its applicability.

For workflow/kit changes follow the governing independent-review process.
Do not use manual moves or direct database changes to evade acceptance gates.

# Execute only approved effects

Task creation and launch are separate effects. Do not assume launch merely
materializes an idle worktree: initial Plan may proceed automatically through
acceptance to Implement. Authorize that scope or identify a supported pause
for genuinely missing authority. Resolve the project's supported transfer route,
task-fixed storage mode and single writer before mutation. Where the route
uses Plan followed by Implement, Plan reads predecessor evidence without product
edits and the accepted Implement writer owns transfer. Do not seed concurrently
with another writer, race Start with Interrupt or invent a prestart
materialization helper. Preserve admitted infrastructure and initialize storage
through the supported project-owned route and qualified adapter. Missing safe
admission is a blocker, not permission to improvise a fallback.

Preserve predecessor roots/history. Create successors through supported tooling.
Recheck relevant states and source preimages before effects. Maintain one
predecessor-to-successor mapping; unknown create outcomes require lookup before
retry. Revalidate changed bindings, not unrelated completed investigation.
Record real predecessor/successor IDs and original authority links.
Verify transferred code and context before treating the successor as ready.
Check that the admitted next route remains usable after the actual handoff;
source-transfer PASS alone does not establish executable continuation. Do not
approve or exercise a product transition merely to prove this. Diagnose a
rejected completion without replaying an unchanged failure; preserve effect
status and use a supported, separately authorized recovery route.
Run only authorized checks; obtain new evidence only where binding changes
require it and authority permits it. Never relabel old receipts as new runs.
Neither assume all old tests remain admissible nor rerun everything merely
because identity changed. Determine checks from actual bindings and next gates.

# Completion

Report per successor: prepared/not created, created, source transfer verified, instructions loaded,
plan accepted, executable, or blocked. These are different states.
Retain the exact rollback and predecessor cleanup obligations.
Close each predecessor only when its required code/context transfer is verified,
restrictions and dependency links are preserved, resource duties are settled
or safely retained by their existing owner, and cancellation is explicitly
authorized. Verify dependency updates before closure can unblock other work.
Do not wait for successor product acceptance or unrelated migrations unless a
real dependency or an explicit human decision requires it. Never bypass
node-owned resource settlement with terminal cancellation.

At the agreed migration endpoint or a concrete blocker handoff, stop only
migration-owned observers and verify they ended. Do not stop product Sessions
or Tasks, and do not silently extend migration into ongoing product supervision.
Report any retained resource or monitoring duties separately.
Rollback stops further effects and preserves verified state; it does not
implicitly authorize deleting Tasks, branches or history.
